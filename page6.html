Choosing the Right PC Case

Choosing a PC case is more important than simply picking the one that looks the best. Your case needs to fit your hardware, provide enough airflow, and leave enough room for future upgrades.

Motherboard Compatibility

One of the first things to check when choosing a case is motherboard compatibility. PC cases are designed to support different motherboard sizes, so make sure your motherboard matches the case you choose.

  • Mini-ITX - designed for very small builds. (We do not currently support this)
  • Micro-ATX - smaller than standard ATX while still offering flexibility.
  • ATX - a common size for gaming and general-purpose PCs.
  • E-ATX/Extended-ATX - larger boards designed for high-end or specialized systems.

GPU and Cooler Clearance

Your graphics card and CPU cooler also need enough physical space inside the case. Large graphics cards and CPU coolers can be surprisingly difficult to fit in smaller cases.

Before buying a case, check its maximum GPU length and CPU cooler height. If you are using a liquid cooler, check the supported radiator sizes and mounting locations as well.

Airflow

A case should allow cool air to enter and warm air to leave. Good airflow helps keep your components at reasonable temperatures, especially during gaming or other demanding workloads.

  • Make sure the case has adequate intake ventilation.
  • Make sure there are enough exhaust fans or vents.
  • Do not block the case's air intakes.
  • Consider how your fans will be positioned before building.

Room for Upgrades

Your PC may not always have the same hardware it started with. A case with additional fan mounts, storage space, and expansion room can make future upgrades much easier.

Planning for future upgrades can save you from replacing the entire case later just because a new component no longer fits.

What Should You Choose?

  • Need a small/travel build? Consider a Mini-ITX or Micro-ATX case.
  • Building a standard gaming PC? An ATX case is often a good choice.
  • Need lots of expansion space? Consider a larger ATX or E-ATX case.
  • Want maximum hardware and absolutely no concern for size? Go big. We certainly won't stop you.

The best case is not necessarily the largest or most expensive. It is the one that fits your hardware, keeps it properly cooled, and gives you enough room to build and upgrade comfortably. And most importantly, it looks good.
