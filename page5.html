Taking Care of Your PC

Whether you are getting a PC or already have one, it is important to know how to properly maintain it. Regular maintenance can help your PC perform well, stay clean, and remain reliable.

Keeping Your PC Clean

When your computer starts getting dusty, and it will, shut down your PC before cleaning it. Use a can of compressed air to remove dust from the case and its components.

When cleaning fans, hold the fan blades in place while using compressed air. This prevents the fan from spinning excessively and reduces unnecessary wear on the fan motor while also preventing backfeeding.

Monitoring Temperatures

Monitoring your CPU temperatures can help you identify problems before they become serious. Software such as Armoury Crate or the NVIDIA App can be used to monitor hardware temperatures.

  • Check CPU temperatures while gaming or under heavy load.
  • Check temperatures while the PC is idle.
  • Watch for temperatures that are unusually high compared with normal operation.
  • Make sure your fans and cooling system are working properly.

Thermal Paste

If your CPU temperatures become unusually high (60°C on idle and 90°C on load), your thermal paste may need to be replaced. Thermal paste helps transfer heat from the CPU to the cooler.

How often thermal paste needs to be replaced depends on the hardware, paste, and usage. There is no universal 2-3 year rule, so check your CPU and cooler manufacturer's recommendations and monitor temperatures before replacing it.

If you need to learn how to replace thermal paste, see the PC building guide.

General Maintenance

  • Keep your PC in a clean, dry location.
  • Make sure air vents are not blocked.
  • Clean dust filters regularly.
  • Keep cables organized so they do not interfere with fans.
  • Keep your operating system and hardware drivers updated.
  • Investigate unusual temperatures, noises, crashes, or shutdowns.

Take Care of Your Hardware

A PC is a $1000+ investment. Keeping it clean, cool, and properly maintained can help your components last longer and keep your system running reliably for even 3-4 generations of GPU. Thats why the GTX 1080 is still S-Tier.
