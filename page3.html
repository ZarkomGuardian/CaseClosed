PC building

Building a PC is a complicated process that can be overwhelming for beginners, many don't know how or where to start assembling one.

Parts

The main parts are as follows.

  • PC case
  • Motherboard
  • CPU (Central Processing Unit)
  • CPU cooler
  • Thermal paste, if required
  • RAM (Random Access Memory)
  • Storage, such as an M.2 NVMe SSD
  • Power supply unit (PSU)
  • Graphics card (GPU), if required
  • Case fans and other accessories, if needed

Assembly

Once you have all the parts, you can start assembling your PC. We have both a video guide from the reputable channel Linus Tech Tips and written instructions. Please note that these two tutorials may differ.

If the embedded video does not load, watch the tutorial on YouTube .

Written Assembly Instructions

Once you have all your components, you can begin assembling your PC. Here are some hard rules ALWAYS work on a clean, stable surface, follow the manuals for your components, and never force a part into a connector or socket.

Step 1: Prepare the Motherboard

Place the motherboard on its cardboard box or another suitable nonconductive surface. Read the motherboard manual before installing any components.

Step 2: Install the CPU

Open the CPU socket according to the motherboard instructions. Align the CPU using the marked corner and place it gently into the socket. Handle the processor carefully and avoid touching its contacts. Close the retention mechanism as instructed. Never force the CPU into place.

Step 3: Install the RAM

Consult your motherboard manual to find the recommended memory slots. For many motherboards with four RAM slots, the recommended slots for two sticks are A2 and B2, usually the second and fourth slots from the CPU. Align each stick with its slot and press evenly until it is fully seated.

Step 4: Install the CPU Cooler

Install the cooler's mounting hardware according to its instructions. Apply thermal paste if it is not already applied to the cooler. The required amount and application method depend on the processor and cooler. Secure the cooler evenly, following the manufacturer's instructions.

Step 5: Install the Storage

If you are using an M.2 NVMe SSD, locate a compatible M.2 slot on your motherboard. Remove its heatsink if necessary, insert the SSD at the appropriate angle, lower it into position, and secure it using the supplied retention mechanism or screw it in with the relevant screw. Reinstall the heatsink if applicable.

Step 6: Prepare the Case

Remove the case panels and check that the motherboard standoffs match the mounting holes of your motherboard. Install the I/O shield if your motherboard requires a separate one. Check that the case has adequate clearance for your cooler, graphics card, and power supply.

Step 7: Install the Motherboard

Carefully lower the motherboard into the case, aligning its ports with the rear I/O opening and its holes with the standoffs. Install the screws and tighten them securely without overtightening.

Step 8: Install the Power Supply

Position the PSU in its designated compartment and secure it with the appropriate screws. Connect the required motherboard, CPU, GPU, and storage power cables. Use only cables compatible with your particular PSU. Never mix modular PSU cables from different models unless compatibility is explicitly confirmed by the manufacturer.

Step 9: Install the Graphics Card

Remove the appropriate expansion-slot covers. Insert the graphics card into the recommended PCIe x16 slot, normally the top reinforced slot. Secure it to the case and connect any required GPU power cables according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Step 10: Connect the Headers and Cables

Connect the case's front-panel power switch, reset switch, LEDs, USB ports, audio connections, and case fans to their corresponding motherboard headers. Use the motherboard manual to identify each connector and its correct orientation. Connect the CPU cooler and its fans to the appropriate headers as well.

Step 11: Check Your Work

Before powering on the PC, check that all components are secure, all required power cables are connected, and no loose screws or cables are obstructing fans. Connect your monitor and peripherals, then power on the system. If it fails to start, turn it off and unplug it before troubleshooting.

What's Next?

This is just the beginning. Once your masterpiece is assembled, you'll need to install an operating system (OS), install the appropriate drivers, and keep your components clean and properly maintained. That would be right down here.
